## 1. Executive Summary
WG21 ships standards on a predictable three-year cadence. Veto dispersion across subgroups combined with high downstream switching costs favors additive complexity over structural simplification.

## 2. The Subject
Founded in 1989 by Bjarne Stroustrup, [The Committee - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/the-committee) operates as ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21.

## 4. Structural Assessment

### The Consensus Lock-in Loop
Switching costs (Klemperer 1987) across billions of lines of production code prevent downstream exit while multi-stage subgroup consensus disperses live player (Burja 2020) authority. The specification accretes features faster than it simplifies.

---

## 5. Institutional Health
The prognosis is Functional. The three-year release train delivers ratified standards, though coordination rests on distributed subgroup chairs.

---

## 6. Economic Position
Switching costs anchor enterprise users to the standard toolchain.

## 8. Predictions

### Short-term (0-2 years)
If C++26 ratifies on schedule, vendor parity holds. If not, dialect fragmentation increases (high).

### Medium-term (2-5 years)
If memory-safety profiles ship, regulated sectors retain C++ for new modules. If not, new projects adopt substitutes (medium).

### Long-term (5-10 years)
If subgroup chair succession broadens, review velocity holds. If not, backlog stalls major features (medium).
